If you're reading this in late September, you have about 6 weeks to get your legs ready. If you wait until October, you're already behind.

Here's the harsh truth: Your first day back on skis will hurt. Your quads will burn, your knees will ache, and by run 5 you'll be wondering if you're too old for this. Unless you actually prepare.

This isn't about becoming an Olympic athlete. It's about showing up on opening day and skiing all afternoon without your legs giving out by noon. Here's the 8-week plan that actually works.

## Week 1-2: Build a Cardio Base

Skiing is partially disguised as an endurance sport. You're on your feet for 4-6 hours, your heart rate spikes on every run, and your body is working harder than it feels like. If your cardio is trash, you'll gas out early.

What to do:

**Hike, bike, or run 3-4x per week:** 30-45 minutes, moderate intensity. You should be breathing hard but still able to talk.

![Hiking for cardio](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-02.webp)
![Biking for cardio](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-03.webp)

**Stair climber** (if you have gym access): 20-30 minutes, 2-3x per week. This mimics the quad burn of skiing better than running.

![Stair climber exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-04.webp)

**Goal:** Get your heart and lungs used to sustained effort so that your muscles are not competing for all your oxygen levels.

Why it matters: Skiing is partly aerobic. If you can't sustain 30 minutes of cardio, you're not skiing a full day without stopping every 3 runs to rest your precious legs.

## Week 3-4: Leg Strength (Quads, Glutes, Hamstrings)

This is where the real work starts. Skiing destroys your quads. If they're not strong, they'll burn out in the first hour, and you'll spend the rest of the day in survival mode.

Exercises (3x per week, full-body or lower-body focus):

**1. Squats (bodyweight or weighted):**

![Squat exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-05.webp)

3 sets of 12-15 reps. Go as low as you can while keeping your knees tracking over your toes. This builds quad and glute strength for the loading phase of turns.

**2. Lunges (forward, reverse, or walking):**

![Lunge exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-06.webp)

3 sets of 10 per leg. Mimics the single-leg loading you do while skiing. Bonus: Helps with balance.

**3. Wall Sits:**

![Wall sit exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-07.webp)

3 sets of 45-60 seconds. Hold a squat position with your back against a wall, knees at 90 degrees. This is brutal, but it's the closest thing to the sustained quad burn of skiing.

**4. Step-Ups (on a bench or box):**

![Step-up exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-08.webp)

3 sets of 12 per leg. Builds single-leg power for getting out of turns.

**5. Glute Bridges:**

![Glute bridge exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-09.webp)

3 sets of 15. Strengthens glutes and hamstrings (often neglected, but critical for stability).

Why it matters: Your quads, glutes, and hamstrings are the primary movers in skiing. If they're weak, your knees take over, and that's how you get injured.

## Week 5-6: Balance, Agility, and Single-Leg Work

Skiing is asymmetrical. You're constantly shifting weight from one leg to the other, balancing on edges, and recovering from off-balance moments. If your balance sucks, your skiing suffers.

Exercises (3x per week):

**1. Single-Leg Squats (or pistol squats if you're advanced):**

![Single-leg squat exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-10.webp)

3 sets of 8 per leg. Stand on one leg, squat down as far as you can, and stand back up. Use a chair or wall for balance if needed.

**2. Single-Leg Deadlifts:**

![Single-leg deadlift exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-11.webp)

3 sets of 10 per leg. Stand on one leg, hinge at the hips, reach toward the ground with your opposite hand. Builds hamstring and glute strength while challenging balance.

**3. Bosu Ball or Balance Board Work:**

![Balance board exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-12.webp)

3 sets of 1-minute holds. Stand on an unstable surface (Bosu ball, balance board, even a couch cushion) and maintain balance. Mimics the instability of skiing on uneven terrain.

**4. Lateral Hops (side-to-side):**

![Lateral hop exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-13.webp)

3 sets of 15-20 hops. Jump side to side over a line or small obstacle. Builds lateral power and mimics the side-to-side loading of ski turns.

**5. Box Jumps (optional, if you have access to a plyometric box):**

![Box jump exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-14.webp)

3 sets of 8-10 jumps. Explosive power for quick edge-to-edge transitions.

Why it matters: Skiing is unstable. If you can only squat with both feet planted, you're not training the movements you actually use on the mountain.

## Week 7-8: Ski-Specific Endurance and Lateral Movements

This is the final push. You're simulating the sustained, repetitive loading that skiing demands.

Exercises (3-4x per week):

**1. Skater Hops:**

![Skater hop exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-15.webp)

3 sets of 20 (10 per side). Jump laterally from one leg to the other, landing softly and holding for 1 second. This mimics the lateral push-off and landing of skiing.

**2. Wall Sits (Extended Duration):**

![Extended wall sit exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-16.webp)

3 sets of 90-120 seconds. If you can hold a wall sit for 2 minutes, your quads are ready for opening day.

**3. Jumping Lunges:**

![Jumping lunge exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-17.webp)

3 sets of 12 (6 per leg). Lunge position, jump and switch legs mid-air. Builds explosive power and endurance.

**4. Mountain Climbers:**

![Mountain climber exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-18.webp)

3 sets of 30 seconds. High plank position, drive your knees toward your chest rapidly. Builds core stability and cardio endurance.

**5. Plank Variations (front plank, side plank):**

![Plank exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-19.webp)

3 sets of 60 seconds each. Skiing uses your core more than you think — it stabilizes your upper body while your legs do all the turning.

Why it matters: Skiing is repetitive. You're making 20-30 turns per run, all day long. If your legs can't handle sustained lateral loading, they'll give out.

## The Wall Sit Test: Can You Hold 2 Minutes?

This is the benchmark. If you can hold a wall sit (back against wall, knees at 90 degrees, thighs parallel to the ground) for 2 minutes, your quads are ready for a full day of skiing.

If you can only hold 30-45 seconds, you'll struggle on opening day. Test yourself in Week 1, then retest in Week 8. The improvement will be obvious.

## Flexibility and Mobility: The Forgotten Component

Strong legs are useless if your hips and ankles are tight. Skiing requires a full range of motion — deep knee bends, ankle flex, hip rotation.

Stretches to do daily (5-10 minutes):

**1. Hip Flexor Stretch:**

![Hip flexor stretch](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-20.webp)

Lunge position, push your hips forward. Skiing keeps your hips flexed all day; tight hip flexors lead to lower back pain.

**2. Ankle Mobility (ankle circles, calf stretches):**

![Ankle mobility exercise](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-21.webp)
![Calf stretch](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-22.webp)

Sit on the ground, rotate your ankles 20x each direction. Stretch your calves by leaning into a wall. Tight ankles = can't get forward in your boots.

**3. Hamstring Stretch:**

![Hamstring stretch](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-23.webp)

Sit on the ground, legs straight, reach for your toes. Hold for 30 seconds.

**4. Quad Stretch:**

![Quad stretch](/images/blog/ski-fit-training/exercise-24.webp)

Stand on one leg, pull your heel toward your glute with your hand. Hold for 30 seconds per leg.

## The Bottom Line

You have 8 weeks. That's enough time to build real strength, balance, and endurance — but only if you start now. The exercises here require zero gym membership. You can do most of them in your living room with nothing but your body weight.

Follow this plan, and opening day won't destroy you. Your legs will hold up through the afternoon. You'll ski better, hurt less, and actually enjoy the first runs of the season instead of surviving them.

Now stop reading and go do some squats.
