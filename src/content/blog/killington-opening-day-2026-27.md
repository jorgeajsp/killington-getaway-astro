Killington's projected opening day for the 2026-27 season is November 11, 2026, and if you're planning on showing up that first weekend, get ready to battle through the die-hard locals who are looking to rack up their number of days on the slopes.

Usually you can plan on roughly 5-10 trails of manmade snow, some ice, and the kind of hardpack that'll rattle your fillings if you hit it wrong.

But here's why people still go: smaller crowds, cheaper lodging, and the simple fact that it's days after Halloween and you have bragging credentials to say you have 2-3 days on the slopes already. If you can manage your expectations and know what you're walking into, early season at Killington is a lot of fun.

## What's Actually Open in November

Opening day usually means Superstar and maybe Great Northern if snowmaking cooperates. By Thanksgiving, you might have Cascade, Skye Peak, and a few other trails, but don't expect much more than 10-15% of the mountain.

The K-1 Gondola will be running to get you up to the Superstar base area, but most of the other lifts—Skye Peak Quad, Ramshead, Bear Mountain—won't spin until December or later. This is a Superstar-and-done situation, and if you're hoping to explore the mountain, save it for January.

Terrain parks won't be set up yet. Glades won't be open. The entire Outer Limits section (Killington's gnarly double-blacks) will be roped off. You're skiing groomers, and that's it.

## The Ice Reality: You're Skiing on Manmade Snow

Early season snow in Vermont is 100% manmade. Rarely any powder, no natural accumulation—just the stuff Killington's snowmaking team has been blasting onto trails for the past few weeks. It's dense, it's hard, and when it gets skied off and exposed to a few freeze-thaw cycles, it turns into something closer to a hockey rink than a ski slope.

Your edges matter more in November than any other time of year. If your skis haven't been tuned since last March, you're going to spend the whole day slipping out on turns and wondering why everyone else looks more in control than you. Get a professional tune before opening day—freshly sharpened edges and a proper wax make a huge difference on hardpack.

Also: Avoid backseat skiing. When the snow is hard, sitting back on your skis means you lose edge grip, and that's when you start sliding sideways instead of carving. Stay forward, keep your weight centered over your boots, and commit to your turns. It feels sketchy at first, but it works.

## Crowds and Lift Lines: The Upside of November

One of the best things about early season skiing is how empty the mountain is. You're not fighting weekend warrior traffic, you're not waiting 20 minutes for the K-1 Gondola, and you're definitely not dealing with holiday crowds.

Weekdays in November are borderline deserted. If you can swing a Tuesday or Wednesday trip, you'll practically have the trail to yourself. Even weekends are manageable—nothing like the zoo you get in January or February.

The trade-off: Some of the base area services aren't fully staffed yet. Restaurants might have limited menus or shorter hours, rental shops might not have as many options, and the general vibe is quieter. If you're looking for the full Killington experience with packed lodges and après energy, you won't find it in November. If you just want to ski without dodging a hundred people on every run, this is your time.

## Lodging Deals: November Is 30-40% Cheaper

Here's where early season gets really attractive: Hotels and Airbnbs that charge $300-500/night in February are going for $150-250 in November. Demand is lower, availability is higher, and if you're flexible with dates, you can score some serious deals.

The Killington Grand, Summit Lodge, and Cascades Lodge all drop their rates in November, and Access Road Airbnbs are easier to book without the 3-month-advance planning you need during peak season. Rutland hotels (20 minutes away) are even cheaper—sometimes under $100/night if you're okay with a basic chain.

Pro tip: Book with flexible cancellation. Early season weather is unpredictable, and if opening day gets pushed back (it happens) or conditions are terrible, you want the option to bail without losing your deposit.

## What to Expect: Limited Terrain, Repetitive Runs

If you ski Superstar five times, you've basically skied everything that's open. By the tenth run, you've memorized every turn. This is not a weekend where you're exploring new trails or finding hidden stashes—you're lapping the same groomer over and over.

Some people love this. It's a chance to work on technique, dial in your carving, and shake off the rust from the offseason. You're not thinking about where to go next; you're just skiing.

Other people find it boring. If you need variety to stay engaged, early season might not be your thing. But if you're just psyched to be back on snow, it's enough.

## Midweek Closures: Check the Schedule

In November, Killington sometimes closes midweek if conditions don't justify staying open. This is more common the first week or two, before Thanksgiving. If you're planning a Monday-Wednesday trip, call ahead or check the resort's website to confirm they're actually running lifts.

Thanksgiving Weekend (November 27-29, 2026) is usually a safe bet because the [Killington Cup](https://killington.com/the-killington-cup) is happening, and the resort goes all-in for that event. Plan on watching the best women's downhill skiers in the world. The event is usually televised and brings in close to 10,000 spectators. Live music, food trucks, ski industry vendors and plenty of swag. Parking is free but there is an entry fee to be paid.

## Gear Prep: Tune Your Skis, Check Your Boots

Early season is when you find out your boots maybe don't fit anymore, your bindings need adjustment, or your skis are trashed from last year's spring skiing. Don't wait until you're in the parking lot to discover this.

**Tune your skis:** Edges sharpened, bases waxed, any core shots repaired. Early season ice will eat dull edges alive.

**Check your boots:** If they felt tight last year, they'll feel tighter now. Get them heat-molded or adjusted before you spend a day fighting numb toes.

**Test your bindings:** Make sure they're set to the right DIN and actually release when they should. You don't want to find out mid-run that your binding is stuck.

Rental gear is an option, but early season rental fleets are usually the previous year's stock that hasn't been rotated out yet. If you're renting, go to a shop that actually tunes their gear (not the resort base lodge if you can avoid it).

## Weather: November in Vermont Is a Coin Flip

You could get bluebird 35-degree days with perfect visibility. You could get 15-degree mornings with wind gusts that'll freeze your face off. You could get rain. November weather in Vermont doesn't care about your plans.

Check the forecast obsessively in the days leading up to your trip. If it's calling for rain, reconsider—skiing in the rain on manmade snow is miserable, and the resort might not even open. If it's calling for temps in the 20s and sun, you're golden.

Layer up more than you think you need to. It's colder standing on a lift in November than it is in January because your body isn't used to it yet. Bring a neck gaiter, extra gloves, and hand warmers.

## Why November Skiing Is Worth It (If You Know What You're Getting)

Early season isn't about epic powder days or exploring the mountain. It's about shaking off the offseason, getting your legs back, and remembering what it feels like to be on snow. It's also about beating the crowds and saving money on lodging.

If you go in expecting February conditions, you'll be disappointed. If you go in knowing it's limited terrain on hardpack and you're okay with that, you'll have a great time.

Plus, you get to be one of the first people skiing in the East that season. There's something satisfying about being on snow in November while everyone else is still waiting for winter to start.

## The Downsides

- **Limited terrain:** You're skiing the same run 10+ times.
- **Ice and hardpack:** Barely powder, almost no soft snow, mostly frozen manmade.
- **Midweek closures:** Not guaranteed to be open every day.
- **Lousy temps:** November cold hits different when you're not used to it yet and could warm up into the 50s or 60s pretty quickly.
- **Fewer services:** Some restaurants and shops have limited hours.

If those are dealbreakers, wait until December or January. But if you can live with them, early season is a great way to get back on skis without the February crowds and prices.
